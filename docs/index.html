    The MCP UI Cookbook

    From atomic UI capabilities to native-like applications

    An MCP application is a sandboxed iframe that speaks JSON-RPC to a host it does not trust, on behalf of a user it can see and a model it cannot. This book takes that surface apart: 87 atomic capabilities, the components they compose into, 13 application recipes that put them under load, and a conformance suite that decides whether a host really supports what it claims.

    Every capability entry says where its behaviour comes from: 28 are messages in the MCP Apps extension, 4 come from core MCP, 20 are the browser inside the sandbox, 19 are yours to build, and 16 have no standard mechanism at all. The last group is the most interesting part of the book.

    Pinned to core protocol 2026-07-28, the MCP Apps extension io.modelcontextprotocol/ui, and SDK 1.7.5. The applications on these pages are running, not recorded. Repository.

    Part I - Foundations

    Chapter 1
    The Capability Model
    Eighty-seven named operations, four conformance levels, five kinds of ground, and one question you have to ask before using any of them.
     Chapter 2
    What Crosses the Boundary
    The whole wire protocol in one chapter: the handshake, the twenty messages, the double iframe, and the four ways a view finds out what it was rendered for.
     Chapter 3
    One View at a Time
    The unit of rendering is the tool call. There is no page, nothing owns a screen, and two views cannot share one. Most of the hard problems in this book descend from that.
     Chapter 4
    The Local-First Principle
    Sorting a column is not a conversation. Where the agent boundary goes, why it goes there, and what it costs when it goes somewhere else.
     Chapter 5
    The Sandbox and What It Costs You
    An opaque origin, a content security policy your server declares and the host enforces, four permissions, and the surprising consequences of all of it.
     Chapter 6
    Entries, Recipes, and the Discipline of Degradation
    The two templates the rest of the book is written to, the five failure modes every capability has, and why the failure section is the one that earns its keep.

    Part II - Atomic UI capabilities

    Chapter 7
    Surfaces and Geometry
    How to size your view, read the space you were given, go fullscreen, and ask to be closed. Most embedded layout bugs are in this chapter.
     Chapter 8
    Focus and Scroll
    Inside the frame, focus and scrolling work exactly as they always have. Across the frame boundary, neither of them works at all, and the protocol has nothing to say about it.
     Chapter 9
    Dialogs and Attention
    Six capabilities, no messages, one broken browser API, and a decision procedure for how loudly to interrupt somebody.
     Chapter 10
    Keyboard, Pointer and Selection
    Nine capabilities, one message between them. The browser does almost all of this, which is why the two places it stops are worth naming.
     Chapter 11
    Clipboard, Files and System Integration
    Two messages that exist because the sandbox closed a door, one permission, and three capabilities with no route at all.
     Chapter 12
    Pickers and Dropzones
    Three inputs the browser has already built for every platform and locale, and one picker that has to come through the host because the view holds no connection.
     Chapter 13
    Host Environment and Theming
    A theming contract in three layers, seven environment signals, and the reason your view must define every colour it uses even though the host is going to send them.
     Chapter 14
    Accessibility
    Three preferences the host never sends, one live region you have to build, and the reason an embedded frame makes all of this harder than a page.
     Chapter 15
    Lifecycle and Connectivity
    Your view will be destroyed without warning, hidden without notice, and disconnected without an event. One request in the whole protocol gives you time to react.
     Chapter 16
    Persistence and History
    The sandbox took away your storage and the extension has not replaced it. Undo, on the other hand, was always yours, and in an agentic application it matters more than it ever did in a page.
     Chapter 17
    Media, Progress, Cancellation and Errors
    Playback the platform gives you, immersion the host gives you, and four capabilities about work that takes long enough for somebody to change their mind.

    Part III - Agent and UI

    Chapter 18
    UI to Agent
    Three ways for a view to reach the conversation, one of which is quiet, one of which starts a turn, and one of which skips the conversation entirely.
     Chapter 19
    Agent to UI
    The host cannot see inside your frame, so an agent that wants to act on your interface has to be given a vocabulary. Registering tools is that vocabulary, and it turns out to be the same mechanism accessibility and testing need.
     Chapter 20
    Tool Invocation and Streaming
    The view can call server tools without the model's involvement, receive arguments before they finish arriving, and take delivery of a result it was rendered for.
     Chapter 21
    State Ownership and Concurrency
    Two parties can write to the same document and neither knows what the other is doing. Declare who owns what before it happens, because afterwards there is no good default.
     Chapter 22
    Approval, Elicitation and the Context Boundary
    Two capabilities the extension does not have, one principle it depends on, and an argument about what the model should never be told.

    Part IV - Reusable components

    Chapter 23
    Components I: Overlays, Controls and Text
    What each component composes, which ARIA pattern governs it, and the embedded-specific detail that makes it different from the version you have built before.
     Chapter 24
    Components II: Navigation, Data and Visualization
    Structure without a URL bar, tables that survive streaming, and charts in a frame that cannot load a charting library.
     Chapter 25
    Components III: Spatial, Media and Agent-Native
    Canvases in a small frame, transports you should probably not replace, and five components that exist only because there is a model in the room.

    Part V - Application recipes

    Recipe 1
    Data Explorer
    The baseline recipe. A table you can sort, filter, page and select, and one button that turns a selection into a question.
     Recipe 2
    Dashboard
    Tiles, sparklines, drill-down, and values that keep changing while you look at them.
     Recipe 3
    File Explorer
    A tree, a context menu, drag to move, and a deletion that is both confirmed and undoable.
     Recipe 4
    Document Editor
    The recipe where the agent writes too. Undo, autosave through a tool, a teardown that flushes, and a rewrite that has to be proposed rather than applied.
     Recipe 5
    Spreadsheet
    A grid with a formula evaluator, range selection, and a clipboard format chosen for where the data is going.
     Recipe 6
    Image Annotator
    A canvas with pointer capture, zoom about the cursor, a dropzone that validates, and an export that degrades to the clipboard.
     Recipe 7
    Workflow Builder
    A node graph the agent can edit through tools, with validation that runs continuously and an undo stack that covers both parties.
     Recipe 8
    Code and Diff Reviewer
    A unified diff where a range of lines is the unit of conversation, and where the model is asked about exactly what the reviewer selected.
     Recipe 9
    Monitoring Console
    Sustained streaming, a selection that survives it, connectivity reported honestly, and an error that happens while nobody is looking.
     Recipe 10
    Approval Center
    A queue of actions waiting for a person, with the provenance that makes approval meaningful and a tool interface that refuses to approve anything.
     Recipe 11
    Agent Control Center
    Tasks that stream in before they exist, progress against a running request, cancellation that admits how far it got, and a retry that does not make you start over.
     Recipe 12
    Media Viewer
    A transport, a caption track, an immersion request that goes through the host, and a share button for a capability that does not exist.
     Recipe 13
    Settings and Preferences
    A form, done properly. Native pickers, validation that waits until you have finished typing, save and discard that mean what they say, and a dirty guard on teardown.

    Part VI - Verification and conformance

    Chapter 26
    Why Recipes Are the Test Suite
    Capabilities pass in isolation and fail in composition. The thirteen applications exist to find out which, and the completeness test is what they answer.
     Chapter 27
    The Verification Layers
    Six layers, from a schema to a cross-host dashboard, and an honest account of which of them this repository actually runs.
     Chapter 28
    The Coverage Matrix and the Checklists
    What eighty-seven capabilities by thirteen recipes actually shows, why the matrix is generated, and what a host is claiming when it claims a level.
     Chapter 29
    The Missing Primitive Loop, and the Live Book
    How a gap gets found, what a good proposal looks like, and how a static site runs thirty applications next to their own source without hosting anything.
