@recipe r09

The recipe that runs over time, and the one that exposes `tool.partialOutput`
as the most consequential gap in Part III. Everything in it is about staying
correct while data keeps arriving.

@demo r09-monitoring-console

## What stays local, what crosses

| Normally local | Worth crossing |
|---|---|
| Follow the tail, or stop following | "What caused this burst of resets?" |
| Select a line | "Is this error related to the deploy?" |
| Filter by service | "Summarise the last ten minutes" |
| Reconnect | |

## Appending, never rebuilding

The single most important line in this recipe is the one that is not there:
the list is never replaced.

<!-- listing: illustrative -->
```js
$("#log").append(node);
while ($("#log").children.length > 220) $("#log").firstChild.remove();
if ($("#follow").checked) $("#log").scrollTop = $("#log").scrollHeight;
```

Appending and trimming preserves selection, focus and scroll position.
Replacing the container's children destroys all three, and at five lines a
second the user notices immediately. Press "Stream 40 more lines" with a line
selected: the selection stays.

## Following, and stopping

Follow the tail until the user scrolls up, then stop and leave the checkbox
unticked. Every terminal has taught people this behaviour, and it is the kind
of thing nobody notices when it is right.

## Streaming without a streaming mechanism

There is no partial tool result. What this recipe does is receive repeated
`ui/notifications/tool-result` notifications, each carrying a batch, which
works and is not something the specification promises. Chapter 20 has the gap
and the three workarounds. This recipe uses the one that produces the best
interface and is the least portable, and says so.

## Connectivity, honestly

Three states: live, reconnecting, offline. The critical design decision is
what happens to the data in the last two. The console keeps the lines it has
and raises a banner saying the view is showing the last lines that arrived,
which is not the current state.

A console that clears itself when the connection drops has destroyed the
evidence at the exact moment somebody needs it.

## The error nobody saw

When an error line arrives while `document.hidden` is true, the console raises
a banner that survives until the user comes back, and publishes the alert to
the model's context. Neither is a substitute for `notify.attention`, which
does not exist. Both are what you can do instead.

## Try this

**Select a log line, then press "Stream 40 more lines".** The selection
survives, because the list appends and trims and never rebuilds.

**Scroll up while lines are arriving.** Following stops and the checkbox
unticks itself, which is the behaviour every terminal has taught people.

**Watch the rate chip.** It is the only honest signal that the buffer is
dropping lines during a burst.

## What it needs from the host

| Level | Needs | Without it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Render, resize, theme | Nothing works |
| 3 | `lifecycle.visibility` | The console cannot tell it was backgrounded |
| 3 | `notify.attention` | Gap; the banner-on-return is the fallback |
| 4 | `tool.invoke` | No log to tail |
| 4 | `tool.partialOutput` | Gap; repeated results are the fallback |
| 4 | `agent.contextUpdate` | The model does not learn about alerts |

## What happens when things go wrong

**The frame is hidden by CSS.** No `visibilitychange` fires, the console keeps
rendering, and nothing tells it otherwise. Chapter 15 has this gap; the
mitigation here is that rendering a log line is cheap.

**A burst larger than the buffer.** The buffer is 220 lines, so a burst of a
thousand loses the oldest. The rate chip is what tells the user this is
happening.

## Keyboard and screen reader

The log is `role="log"`, which is a live region with polite semantics, so new
lines are announced without interrupting. At high rates that is unusable,
which is a real tension: the correct behaviour at five lines a second is
probably to announce a summary, and this recipe does not
do that. It is recorded as a known limitation rather than claimed as done.
Lines carry their level in text as well as colour. Selection is by click and
by keyboard.

## Conformance checks

1. `tail_logs` returns 24 lines and all render.
2. A selected line stays selected after 40 more lines arrive.
3. Scrolling up unticks Follow; scrolling to the bottom does not re-tick it.
4. Going offline raises a banner and keeps the existing lines.
5. An error line arriving while hidden raises a banner visible on return.
6. The buffer never exceeds 220 elements.
